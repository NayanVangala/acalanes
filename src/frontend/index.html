3The method

How the simulator works

  1. Real records: 300,081 San Francisco 911 calls, with when each unit was dispatched and when it arrived.
  2. Learned travel times: the typical drive from each station to each 500 m block, by hour, from 333,000 real trips.
  3. Simulator: replays every call in order. The call's own station goes first when it's free; otherwise the unit that can arrive soonest. Busy units stay busy.
  4. Checked against reality: a month the model never saw (October 2026). By neighborhood it matches real arrival times with r = 0.94.

No AI calculates or predicts these numbers. Claude Haiku is used only to read questions typed below.

4Does it work?

How accurate is this?

We replayed October 2026, a month the model never saw, and compared it with the real recorded arrival times. "Area r" is how closely neighborhood medians match reality (1.0 is perfect).

6Limits

  • A correlation, not a cause. The equity gap matches distance from stations; we didn’t test other causes.
  • Areas, not single calls. Neighborhood medians are within about 16 s of reality, but one call can be off by about 1.5 min.
  • One month of testing. The model was checked on October 2026 only.
  • Fire and EMS only. No police response, hospital capacity, traffic data or road closures; slower driving is a scaling factor, not real traffic.
  • The AI only reads words. Claude Haiku turns a typed situation into a plan; the simulator produces every number.

        Medio

        When you call 911, the minutes until help arrives matter. Medio replays a real day of San Francisco 911 calls: fire engines, ambulances and police. Each fire or medical call goes to the engine or ambulance that can get there soonest. Travel times are learned from 333,000 real trips, so hills and traffic are already in them.

        Vehicles follow San Francisco’s street centerlines (DataSF); how long each trip takes comes from the model. Police cars are replayed from real SFPD dispatch records, not simulated. Hospitals show how many hours each spent on ambulance diversion (not accepting ambulances) in the last 12 months. Click an engine or ambulance to follow it. Change the city on the left, then run it again.

        Limits: no crew staffing changes, no help from nearby cities, no hospital wait times. Use it to compare neighborhoods and options, not to close a real station on its own.
